The core solver



Try it: start at low Rayleigh number and add a heat pulse. Does it fade or grow into circulation? Raise Rayleigh number to strengthen convection, then heat from above and compare the response. A small initial temperature disturbance seeds the experiment reproducibly.


Warm fluid is buoyant and cold fluid tends to sink. When the bottom is hot, rising and falling plumes can form circulation rolls. With the top hot, the stratification is stable. Prandtl number changes the balance between momentum diffusion and heat diffusion while Rayleigh number is held fixed.


Nu compares heat crossing the plates with conduction alone. Nu = 1 is the conductive reference. The displayed value averages the bottom and top plate fluxes; these can differ while the fluid gains or loses heat. Pulses create additional transients.

Two-dimensional Rayleigh–Bénard convection, periodic from left to right, with stationary fixed-temperature plates. D2Q9 moment collision for flow and D2Q5 thermal transport, coupled by Boussinesq buoyancy. Plate spacing: 64 lattice cells. Free-fall velocity Uff = 0.07 limits compressibility; Ra and Pr set ν and α. The grid gives a qualitative view at the upper end of the range; thin boundary layers need finer resolution.
