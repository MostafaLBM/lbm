The core solver



Try it: lower the pressure and compare a wetting surface with a non-wetting one. Contact angle is measured through the blue fluid; lower angles mean stronger wetting. Watch which throats the blue fluid enters, where it slows, and whether pockets of orange fluid become isolated. Raise the pressure to push the front farther through the rock.


Surface tension resists interface deformation. Narrow throats impose a capillary barrier whose size depends on wetting. Pressure is imposed at the open reservoirs; there is no gravity or uniform body-force drive.


Saturation is the blue fluid’s share of the pore space inside the rock sample. Breakthrough means its φ = 0.5 contour has reached the end of the sample. Isolated pockets are orange regions disconnected from the outlet at that threshold; they can still change as the flow evolves.


Two immiscible fluids with equal density and viscosity. This two-dimensional phase-field model uses a four-cell diffuse interface and nominal wetting on lattice-resolved grain surfaces. Very thin films, tiny pockets and apparent contact angles depend on resolution. Viscosity ν = 0.03; surface tension σ = 0.008.
